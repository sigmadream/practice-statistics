## Abstract

This book is the first installment in a three-part series designed to guide social scientists from foundational statistical concepts to advanced data modeling and analysis using R. It introduces readers to core techniques in statistics and R programming, providing a foundation for subsequent volumes. The second book, _Using R for Intermediate Statistics in the Social Sciences_, builds on these foundations to cover multivariate designs, including factorial, mixed, and repeated measures ANOVA, expanded regression models, and reliability and validity testing. The final volume, _Using R for Advanced Statistics in the Social Sciences_, advances to hierarchical and longitudinal modeling, robust and Bayesian approaches, and contemporary machine learning methods. Collectively, the series offers a structured progression, enabling readers to develop statistical fluency and apply R effectively in social science research, from basic analyses to sophisticated modeling and theory-driven inquiry.

## 11.1 Background

This book is the first in a three-part series designed to guide readers from foundational statistical concepts to advanced data modeling and analysis using R. The next book, _Using R for Intermediate Statistics in the Social Sciences_, will build upon the concepts introduced here, extending them to more complex multivariate models and group comparison designs. The final volume, _Using R for Advanced Statistics in the Social Sciences_, will focus on deeper regression frameworks, hierarchical and longitudinal modeling, and contemporary approaches in machine learning and data science. Both follow-up books will be published under Springer Nature.

## 11.2 Intermediate Statistics

The _Intermediate Statistics_ book will expand the reader’s toolkit from single-predictor and single-outcome models to multivariate designs, or those involving multiple predictors, multiple outcomes, or both. These models allow researchers to test more sophisticated hypotheses common in social science research, including interactions, moderation, mediation, and models that account for both categorical and continuous predictors. The intermediate volume will include three primary sections: group comparison tests, expanded regression models, and reliability and validity analysis.

### 11.2.1 Group Comparison Tests

This book introduced one-way ANOVA, which tests group differences on a single continuous outcome using one categorical independent variable. The intermediate volume will expand upon this foundation by exploring models with multiple independent and/or dependent variables, including:

* **Factorial ANOVA**—One continuous dependent variable with two or more categorical independent variables; allows for the examination of main and interaction effects.
* **ANCOVA (Analysis of Covariance)**—One continuous dependent variable predicted by both categorical and continuous independent variables (the latter serving as covariates for statistical control).
* **Mixed ANOVA**—Combines within-subjects (repeated measures) and between-subjects factors, enabling researchers to analyze changes over time while accounting for group effects.
* **MANOVA (Multivariate ANOVA)**—Two or more continuous dependent variables with one or more categorical independent variables.  
   * _Factorial MANOVA_: Multiple dependent and independent variables analyzed simultaneously.  
   * _Repeated Measures MANOVA_: Continuous dependent variables measured across two or more time points.  
   * _Mixed MANOVA_: Combines repeated measures and factorial structures.  
   * _MANCOVA (Multivariate Analysis of Covariance)_: Adds one or more covariates to control for continuous variables while testing multiple dependent outcomes.

These designs introduce multivariate thinking—understanding how multiple outcomes and predictors interact within the same analytical framework.

### 11.2.2 Expanded Regression Models

While this book introduced bivariate, multiple, and logistic regression, the next volume will expand to more specialized regression frameworks commonly used in the social sciences. These include:

* **Hierarchical (Sequential) Regression**—Examining changes in model fit as predictors are entered in conceptually meaningful blocks.
* **Polynomial and Nonlinear Regression**—Modeling curvilinear relationships and growth patterns.
* **Ordinal and Multinomial Logistic Regression**—Extending logistic regression to ordered and categorical outcomes with more than two levels.
* **Poisson and Negative Binomial Regression**—Modeling count data and addressing overdispersion.
* **Model Selection and Evaluation**—Using AIC, BIC, and cross-validation to assess model fit and prevent overfitting.
* **Interaction and Moderation Analysis**—Testing how relationships between variables change across levels of another predictor.

These models will help readers transition from basic prediction and explanation toward model comparison, diagnostics, and theory-driven analysis.

### 11.2.3 Validity and Reliability Tests

The intermediate book will also introduce tests of **scale reliability and construct validity** in R. These tools are essential for developing and evaluating instruments used in surveys, education, psychology, and related fields. Topics include:

* **Cronbach’s Alpha**—A measure of internal consistency reliability.
* **McDonald’s Omega**—A more robust alternative to alpha for multidimensional constructs.
* **Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA)**—Identifying underlying factor structures in observed variables.
* **Principal Components Analysis (PCA)**—Data reduction and dimensionality simplification for correlated variables.

More advanced measurement approaches, such as confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) and item response theory (IRT), as well as structural equation modeling (SEM) will appear in a later companion text focused on _Scale Development and Psychometrics in R_.

## 11.3 Advanced Statistics

The third book in the series, _Using R for Advanced Statistics in the Social Sciences_, will take a deeper dive into modern modeling approaches and statistical theory. It will focus on models that handle hierarchical, nested, and longitudinal data structures, as well as computational techniques that extend beyond traditional inferential frameworks. Planned topics include:

* **Hierarchical Linear Modeling (HLM)/Multilevel Models**
* **Growth Curve and Longitudinal Analysis**
* **Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE)**
* **Robust and Bayesian Regression Approaches**
* **Machine Learning Applications in R** (e.g., regularization, decision trees, ensemble methods)

Together, these volumes will form a progression, from foundational understanding, to practical modeling, to advanced applied research, designed for social scientists who wish to build both their statistical fluency and their command of R as a research tool.
