Regression can thus be used to predict incomes based on age… … or to predict if heavy cars use more petrol, where “X” equals the weight of the car and “Y” equals kilometres per gallon.

In 1925, **R.A. Fisher** (1890–1962) reconstructed Pearson’s notation, introducing Y = a + bX (the general equation for a straight line) and incorporating the terms “dependent” variable and “independent” variable. This was an essential distinction to make for regression, because the independent variable is the predictor and the dependent variable is the criterion.

Fisher then produced the equation for the regression (or predicted) line: Y’ = a + bX (where b is the regression coefficient and Y’, pronounced “Y prime”, indicates a regression line).
