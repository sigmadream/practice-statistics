## The Statistical Society of London

The lack of official registration was the driving force in the formation of the Statistical Society of London (now the Royal Statistical Society) in 1834\. Malthus, together with the Belgian statistician and meteorologist **Adolphe Quetelet** (1796–1874) and **Charles Babbage** (1791–1871), who developed the universal calculating machine (a precursor of the computer), joined forces to establish the Society.

![image](./images/00032.jpeg)

Our first recommendation was for a national registration system with a central office in London. Following legislation in 1836, civil registration was introduced, requiring the notification of births, marriages and deaths.

The General Register Office (GRO) was established, giving England and Wales a system of demographic recording unique in Europe at that time. The first comprehensive census was not undertaken in England until 1851, when provisions were made to include age, sex, occupation and birthplace, as well as counting the blind and the deaf.
