## The Pearsonian Family of Curves

By calculating the method of moments, Pearson also provided a variety of theoretical curves in varying graduations, which could then be superimposed onto an empirical curve to determine which gave the best “fit”. These curves were referred to as the “Pearsonian family of curves”.

![image](./images/00141.jpeg)

The more important ones that remain an essential part of theoretical statistics today include:

_**Type III**, the Gamma Curve, which he went on to use for finding the exact chi-square distribution (discussed later)_ 
_**Type IV**, the Family of Asymmetric Curves (created for Weldon’s data)_ 
_**Type V**, the Normal Curve_ 
_**Type VII**, now known as Student’s distribution for t-tests (examined later)_

![image](./images/00142.jpeg)

Pearson’s family of curves did much to dispel the almost religious acceptance of the normal distribution as the mathematical model of variation of biological, physical and social phenomena.

![image](./images/00143.jpeg)
