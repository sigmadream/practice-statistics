## Malthusian Populations

While various commentators tried to determine the population of a country or the world, the economist **Thomas Robert Malthus** (1766–1834) argued in his celebrated work _An Essay on the Principle of Population_ (1798) that unchecked human populations would always exceed the means of subsistence and that human improvement depended on stern limits on reproduction.

![image](./images/00028.jpeg)

If the population increases while food supplies increase more slowly, there will be a “struggle for existence” in which the fittest individuals will survive and breed. This was a phrase that I later adopted for my theory of natural selection.

Malthus believed that populations would increase exponentially (2, 4, 8, 16, 32, etc.), whereas food supplies would increase mathematically (2, 4, 6, 8, 10, etc.). Malthus’ hypothesis implied that the actual population would always have a tendency to push above the food supply.
