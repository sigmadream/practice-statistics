## Halley’s Mortality Tables

The exceptional work on mortality data in the 18th century was the construction of the Life Table (sometimes referred to as the “Table of Vitality”). This was first suggested by John Graunt and conceived by the astronomer **Edmond Hailey** (1656–1742), best known for his eponymous comet.

![image](./images/00026.jpeg)

I produced the first table of cometary elements in 1676 and constructed the first scientific mortality table in 1693.

The Dutch astronomer and political arithmetician **Nicolaas Struyck** (1687–1769) built on Halley’s work on comets and his research into determining the size of various populations. Struyck organized large-scale population surveys in the Netherlands, but his greatest ambition was to make a reasoned estimate of the total number of people on earth. He wanted to know if the population was increasing, stable or decreasing.
